## Case: Liu Xiaoyan v. Shanghai Century Property Management Co.

**Court**: Putuo District People's Court, Shanghai
**Trial date**: Early May 2026
**Claim**: Refund 6083.14 yuan in hot water fees (2018.09-2024.06)
**Real objective**: Get court ruling confirming hot water equipment belongs to all homeowners (not property company), establishing precedent for community-wide action

## Key Facts
- Property company is subsidiary of the developer
- Community: Shengtian Di, 1046 units total
- Hot water fee: 42 yuan/month flat rate per unit
- Energy management contract: 15yr/168万 with third-party, signed 2014, no homeowner authorization
- Revenue split: property 30-50% / third party 50-70%, homeowners get nothing
- Property service contract Article 31 + Appendix 3: explicitly lists hot water system as "shared facilities"
- Olina moved in 2018; renovation happened in 2014 — no personal before/after comparison
- Refund logic: labor costs should be in property fees + energy savings from unauthorized renovation
- **NEW**: Property forcibly removed her hot water meter during litigation, causing hot water cutoff; she had to self-fund water heater installation

## Strategy
- Two-track approach: procedural violation (no homeowner vote) as primary attack; property ownership as deeper guide
- Three "self-high" traps identified: over-reliance on contract Article 31 alone; assuming procedural violation = automatic refund; assuming judge will proactively rule on ownership
- Mediation red line: property must acknowledge shared ownership in writing; otherwise insist on judgment
- Need to add claims: damages for meter removal + restoration of hot water

## Olina's 7 Key Clarifications (2026-04-09)
1. Learned about renovation only in 2024 from property's public document; doesn't dispute price level, disputes legality of renovation and cost allocation
2. Statute of limitations: knew in late 2024, sued June 2025; fallback: pursue last 3 years only
3. **"Dilemma" argument (strongest point)**: doesn't challenge property's right to collect fees, challenges pricing logic. If it's "business operation" — where's the license, procurement contract, service standards, price standards, hygiene standards, regulatory oversight, authorized remedies?
4. Property's own cost breakdown explicitly includes labor costs and profit-sharing — these are the specific disputed items
5. Strategy: challenge cost calculation to reverse-engineer the ownership question
6. Homeowners only learned about the potentially illegal renovation 2 years ago
7. **All hot water fees fully paid before filing suit** — no arrears whatsoever
